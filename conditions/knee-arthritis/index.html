Knee Arthritis Treatment

Every option for an arthritic knee, from therapy and injections to the MISHA Knee System and MAKO partial or total replacement, with one surgeon in Port St. Lucie and Palm Beach Gardens.

Schedule a Consultation 561-202-8886

What knee arthritis is

Osteoarthritis is the cartilage on the ends of the bones wearing thin. The knee gets stiff in the morning and sore by evening, it may swell or grind, and over time it loses movement. Wear is often uneven: bow-legged alignment, for instance, loads the inner side more than the outer.

Worn cartilage does not grow back by itself. Treatment is about pain, function and, where possible, slowing the wear, and the right option depends on how much of the knee is affected and how you want to use it.

Matching the treatment to the knee

Roughly in the order they are usually considered:

1. Non-surgical care

Physical therapy, weight management, activity changes, bracing, medication and injections. For early to moderate arthritis, orthobiologics such as PRP are another option.

2. The MISHA Knee System

For early to moderate arthritis on the inner side, once non-surgical care has stopped working. A small shock absorber takes load off the worn compartment. Nothing is removed and a replacement remains available later. Dr. Matarazzo was the first surgeon in Florida to implant it. About MISHA.

3. Partial knee replacement

For arthritis confined to one compartment. Only that part is resurfaced, with the MAKO robotic arm. About partial knee replacement.

4. Total knee replacement

For wear across the joint. Total knee replacement, which can also be performed with MAKO.

Why one surgeon for every option matters

Dr. Matarazzo offers each of these, so the recommendation is not shaped by what a practice happens to do. If waiting is the better course, he will say so. If the knee needs a replacement, he will say that too.

What to expect at your first visit

  • An exam of alignment, movement and stability
  • X-rays to see which compartments are worn and how far
  • A plain explanation of where your knee sits on that ladder
  • Non-surgical care first wherever it is likely to help
  • Surgery discussed only when it is the right next step
  • Bring any earlier imaging, the images and not only the report
