Minimally Invasive Procedures

By Marc F. Matarazzo, MD · November 27, 2024

Minimally invasive orthopedic procedures are designed to reduce pain, speed up recovery, and restore mobility with smaller incisions and less disruption to surrounding tissue. These techniques are commonly used for knee, shoulder, and joint conditions related to sports injuries, arthritis, or wear and tear. Below are procedures that may be an option for patients looking to return to an active lifestyle with less downtime.

Arthroscopy (Knee, Shoulder, Hip, Ankle, and Elbow)

Arthroscopy uses a small camera inserted through tiny incisions to diagnose and treat joint issues. It can repair ligament tears (ACL, meniscus, rotator cuff), remove damaged cartilage, and smooth out bone spurs — with less scarring, faster recovery, and reduced pain compared to open surgery.

PRP (Platelet-Rich Plasma) Therapy

PRP therapy uses a patient's own concentrated platelets to promote natural healing and tissue repair. It's commonly used for tendon injuries, ligament tears, and osteoarthritis in the knee, shoulder, and other joints, and may delay or prevent the need for surgery.

Stem Cell Therapy (Regenerative Medicine)

Stem cell therapy involves harvesting stem cells from the patient — typically from bone marrow or fat — and injecting them into the damaged area to promote natural regeneration, helping repair cartilage, tendons, and ligaments.

Ultrasound-Guided Injections

Ultrasound-guided injections deliver corticosteroids, hyaluronic acid, or biologics (PRP, stem cells) directly into the affected joint or tendon for precise treatment — commonly used for shoulder impingement, knee osteoarthritis, and bursitis.

Meniscus Repair & Partial Meniscectomy (Knee)

Meniscus repair uses arthroscopy to stitch torn cartilage together, while a partial meniscectomy trims damaged cartilage while preserving as much healthy tissue as possible. Both restore knee function and joint stability.

Rotator Cuff & Labrum Repair (Shoulder)

Arthroscopic technique repairs torn tendons such as the rotator cuff, or stabilizes the shoulder by fixing the labrum — resulting in less pain, improved range of motion, and faster rehabilitation than traditional surgery.

Partial Knee Replacement (Minimally Invasive Robotic-Assisted Surgery)

Partial knee replacement preserves healthy bone by replacing only the damaged portion of the knee using robotic-assisted technology for enhanced precision, faster recovery, and more natural knee movement.

Shoulder Labral & Biceps Tenodesis Repair

Labrum repair and biceps tenodesis fix labrum tears or reattach the biceps tendon to relieve pain and improve strength, restoring shoulder function while minimizing surgical trauma.
