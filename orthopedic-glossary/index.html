Orthopedic Glossary

The words you will hear in an orthopedic visit, explained in plain English. Each entry links to the page that covers it in more depth.

A

ACL (anterior cruciate ligament)
A ligament in the center of the knee that stops the shin bone sliding forward under the thigh bone and helps control twisting. It is commonly torn in pivoting sports. More on ACL (anterior cruciate ligament)
ACL reconstruction
Surgery that replaces a torn ACL with a graft, usually performed arthroscopically through small incisions. More on ACL reconstruction
Arthritis
Inflammation of a joint. In orthopedics the word most often means osteoarthritis, the wearing away of the cartilage that lines the joint. More on Arthritis
Arthroplasty
The surgical term for replacing or reconstructing a joint, as in total knee arthroplasty (total knee replacement). More on Arthroplasty
Arthroscopy
Surgery performed through small incisions with a camera (the arthroscope) and slim instruments, so the surgeon can see and work inside the joint without opening it fully. More on Arthroscopy
Articular cartilage
The smooth, slippery tissue covering the ends of bones inside a joint. It has almost no blood supply, which is why damage to it rarely heals on its own. More on Articular cartilage
Autograft and allograft
Tissue used to rebuild a ligament or fill a defect. An autograft comes from your own body; an allograft comes from a donor.

B

Board certification
Certification by a specialty board after residency training and examinations. For orthopedic surgeons in the United States the board is the American Board of Orthopaedic Surgery. More on Board certification
Bursitis
Inflammation of a bursa, one of the small fluid-filled sacs that cushion tendons and muscles where they pass over bone.

C

Cartilage restoration
Procedures that repair or replace damaged articular cartilage, such as microfracture, an osteochondral graft or MACI. More on Cartilage restoration
CME (compulsory medical examination)
A formal medical assessment required for a legal, insurance or employment purpose, performed by a physician who is not treating the person examined. More on CME (compulsory medical examination)
Corticosteroid (cortisone) injection
An anti-inflammatory medicine injected into or near a joint to calm pain and swelling. It treats symptoms rather than the underlying damage.
CT scan
Computed tomography: a series of X-rays combined into detailed cross-sectional images of bone. MAKO robotic surgery is planned on a CT scan of your joint. More on CT scan

E

Effusion
Excess fluid inside a joint, often called “water on the knee.” It is a sign that something in the joint is irritated or injured.

F

Fellowship
Additional training of a year or more after residency, focused on one area of a specialty, such as sports medicine and arthroscopy. More on Fellowship

I

IME (independent medical examination)
An examination by a physician who is not treating the patient, requested by an insurer, employer or attorney to answer questions about an injury. It is an assessment, not treatment. More on IME (independent medical examination)
Implantable shock absorber
A device placed outside the knee joint, on its inner side, that takes load off a worn medial compartment without removing bone or cartilage. The MISHA Knee System is one. More on Implantable shock absorber

L

Ligament
A tough band of tissue that connects one bone to another and keeps a joint stable. More on Ligament

M

MACI
Matrix-induced autologous chondrocyte implantation. Some of your own cartilage cells are taken, grown in a laboratory, and implanted on a membrane into a cartilage defect. More on MACI
MAKO robotic-assisted surgery
A robotic-arm system made by Stryker and used in joint replacement. The operation is planned on a 3D model built from a CT scan, and the surgeon performs it with the arm keeping the instruments to that plan. More on MAKO robotic-assisted surgery
Medial and lateral
Medial means the inner side of a joint, toward the middle of the body; lateral means the outer side.
Medial compartment
The inner part of the knee joint, where the inner end of the thigh bone meets the shin bone. More on Medial compartment
Meniscus
One of two C-shaped cartilage cushions between the thigh bone and the shin bone in each knee. Tears cause pain, swelling, catching or locking. More on Meniscus
Meniscus repair and meniscectomy
Two ways of treating a torn meniscus. A repair stitches the tear so it can heal; a meniscectomy trims away the torn part. More on Meniscus repair and meniscectomy
MISHA Knee System
An implantable shock absorber for osteoarthritis on the inner side of the knee. The FDA authorized it in April 2023. More on MISHA Knee System
MRI
Magnetic resonance imaging, which shows soft tissue such as ligaments, tendons, the meniscus and cartilage in detail, without radiation.

O

Orthobiologics
Treatments that use substances from your own body, such as concentrated platelets, to support the repair of tendons, ligaments, cartilage and joints. More on Orthobiologics
Orthopedic surgeon
A physician who specializes in bones, joints, ligaments, tendons and muscles, and treats them both surgically and without surgery. More on Orthopedic surgeon
Osteoarthritis
Gradual wear of the cartilage lining a joint, causing pain, stiffness and swelling. More on Osteoarthritis

P

Partial knee replacement
Surgery that resurfaces only the damaged compartment of the knee and keeps the rest of the joint, including healthy ligaments. Also called unicompartmental knee replacement. More on Partial knee replacement
Patella and patellar dislocation
The patella is the kneecap. A patellar dislocation is when it slips out of its groove at the end of the thigh bone, usually toward the outside of the knee. More on Patella and patellar dislocation
PRP (platelet-rich plasma)
A concentrate of platelets prepared from a sample of your own blood and injected into an injured tendon, ligament or joint. More on PRP (platelet-rich plasma)

R

Reverse shoulder replacement
A shoulder replacement in which the ball and socket swap places, so the deltoid muscle can lift the arm when the rotator cuff no longer works. More on Reverse shoulder replacement
Rotator cuff
Four muscles and their tendons around the shoulder joint that keep the ball centered in the socket and help lift and rotate the arm. More on Rotator cuff

S

Second opinion
A review of your diagnosis and treatment plan by another surgeon, usually before committing to an operation. More on Second opinion
Shockwave therapy
A non-surgical, in-office treatment that delivers acoustic pressure waves to persistent tendon and soft-tissue problems. More on Shockwave therapy
Sports medicine
The diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation and prevention of injuries that happen during sport, exercise and everyday activity. More on Sports medicine

T

Tendon and tendinopathy
A tendon connects muscle to bone. Tendinopathy is a painful, worn or irritated tendon, often from overuse.
Total knee replacement
Surgery that resurfaces the worn ends of the thigh bone, shin bone and usually the kneecap with metal and plastic implants. More on Total knee replacement
Total shoulder replacement
Surgery that replaces the worn ball and socket of the shoulder joint with implants. More on Total shoulder replacement

V

Varus and valgus
Ways a leg can be aligned. Varus is bow-legged, which loads the inner side of the knee; valgus is knock-kneed, which loads the outer side.

W

Workers’ compensation
The insurance system that covers medical care and lost wages for injuries that happen on the job. More on Workers’ compensation
