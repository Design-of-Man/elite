Surgery & Recovery

Surgical reconstruction is typically recommended when non-surgical options aren’t enough to restore stability and function, especially for active patients.

MAKO is a robotic-arm assisted surgical system used for total and partial knee replacement and total shoulder replacement. It allows for more precise bone preparation and implant placement than traditional manual technique, which can support a more natural-feeling joint and a smoother recovery. Dr. Matarazzo is certified in the MAKO system.

Recovery timelines vary by patient and activity goals, but most patients follow a structured rehabilitation protocol over several months before returning to full athletic activity. Dr. Matarazzo builds an individualized, sport-specific rehab plan for every ACL reconstruction patient — ask about your specific timeline during your consultation.

For most surgical procedures, yes — rehabilitation is a large part of the result, not an optional add-on. Dr. Matarazzo prescribes a structured protocol and coordinates with your physical therapist throughout recovery.

Only once you are off sedating pain medication and can control the vehicle safely — which depends on which limb was operated on and whether your car is automatic or manual. Dr. Matarazzo will clear you at a follow-up visit rather than by a fixed calendar date.

Modern implants are durable and many patients get decades of service from them, but no implant is guaranteed for life. Longevity depends on your age, activity level, weight, bone quality, and how the implant is positioned — which is one of the reasons robotic-assisted placement is used.

A partial replacement resurfaces only the damaged compartment of the knee and preserves the rest, including healthy ligaments. A total replacement addresses the whole joint. Partial replacement suits a narrower group of patients — imaging and examination determine which is appropriate for you.

Every surgery carries risk, including infection, blood clots, stiffness, nerve or vessel injury, anaesthetic complications, and the possibility that symptoms are not fully relieved. Dr. Matarazzo will go through the risks specific to your procedure and your health history before you consent.

Non-Surgical Treatment

Shockwave therapy delivers acoustic pressure waves to injured tissue and is used for persistent tendon and soft-tissue problems that have not responded to rest and rehabilitation. It is non-surgical and performed in the office. Whether it suits your condition is decided at consultation.

They are different modalities. Radial shockwave uses pressure waves directed into tissue, while MLS multiwave laser therapy uses specific light wavelengths. Both are non-surgical, in-office treatments used for pain and soft-tissue recovery, and which is appropriate depends on the diagnosis.

Sometimes, and sometimes not — it depends on what is damaged and how badly. Orthobiologics and similar therapies can be effective for certain conditions, but they cannot reattach a torn ligament or resurface a severely arthritic joint. Dr. Matarazzo will be straightforward about what these treatments can and cannot do in your case.

Yes, where clinically appropriate. Injections can be a useful part of a plan, but they treat symptoms rather than the underlying problem, so they are used deliberately and as part of a broader treatment strategy.

Peptide Therapy & Regenerative Medicine

Peptide therapy uses specific, physician-guided compounds (such as BPC-157, TB-500, Ipamorelin, and others) intended to support tissue healing, recovery, and overall wellness. As with any treatment, it should only be pursued under medical supervision — Dr. Matarazzo evaluates whether peptide therapy is appropriate for your specific health profile and goals before prescribing a protocol.

Orthobiologics (such as PRP and other regenerative orthopedic therapies) use substances derived from your own body to support healing at an injury site. Peptide therapy uses specific peptide compounds, often as part of a broader wellness or recovery plan. Dr. Matarazzo can explain which — or which combination — fits your situation during a consultation.

Protocols may involve compounds such as BPC-157, TB-500, Ipamorelin, CJC-1295, GHK-Cu, KPV, MOTS-c, Tesamorelin, and PT-141. Each has a dedicated page on this site. Which — if any — is appropriate for you is a medical decision made at consultation, never from a website.

Regulatory status varies considerably from compound to compound, and many peptides are not FDA-approved for the uses people ask about. That is precisely why this should be physician-supervised. Dr. Matarazzo will discuss the status, the evidence, and the risks of anything he considers for you.

Not necessarily — several peptides are prohibited under anti-doping rules. If you compete under WADA, NCAA, or a league testing policy, say so at your first visit so nothing is prescribed that could jeopardise your eligibility.
