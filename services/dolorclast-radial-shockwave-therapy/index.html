DOLORCLAST® Radial Shockwave Therapy

Focused radial shockwave therapy for chronic musculoskeletal pain, from a certified Guided DolorClast provider in South Florida.

Schedule a Consultation 561-202-8886

High-Energy Tissue Recovery for Orthopedic and Sports Injuries

What is DOLORCLAST® radial shockwave?

DOLORCLAST® delivers radial acoustic pressure waves into tendon, ligament and fascia that have been injured long enough to stop repairing themselves. The waves break that stalled cycle and prompt the tissue back into a healing state — without an incision, a needle, or time away from your week.

Clinically shown to:

  • Ease long-standing tendon pain
  • Raise blood flow and metabolic activity in the tissue
  • Break down calcium deposits and adhesions
  • Prompt the tissue to lay down new collagen
  • Restore movement and function
  • Shorten the road back to activity

What a course looks like

Built on established orthopedic shockwave protocols.

  • Standard course — three to six sessions
  • Spacing — one session a week, which is the interval with the strongest evidence behind it
  • Each session — five to ten minutes
  • Downtime — none. Stay off high-impact exercise for 48 hours; otherwise carry on as normal

How DOLORCLAST® Radial Shockwave Therapy Works

Three Key Mechanisms

Mechanical stimulation — controlled micro-stress wakes up the fibroblasts that rebuild tendon.

New blood vessels — more circulation, more oxygen, more metabolic activity reaching the injury.

Pain relief — overactive pain receptors are interrupted and nerve sensitivity settles back down.

What Patients Feel

A firm, rapid tapping. Over a sore tendon it is intense, but it lasts minutes and people tolerate it well. The soreness usually drops off noticeably within the first few sessions.

Benefits of DOLORCLAST® Radial Shockwave Therapy

It suits orthopedic and sports patients dealing with:

  • Long-standing tendinopathy — Achilles, patellar, gluteal, hamstring or rotator cuff
  • Plantar fasciitis and heel pain
  • Tennis or golfer’s elbow
  • Pain at the side of the hip
  • Calcific tendonitis
  • Trigger points and myofascial pain
  • Shin splints
  • Chronic soft-tissue adhesions
  • Scar tissue or tightness left by an old injury
