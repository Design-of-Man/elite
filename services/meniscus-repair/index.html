Meniscus Repair

Arthroscopic meniscus repair that preserves cartilage and protects long-term knee health. Board-certified orthopedic care in South Florida.

Schedule a Consultation 561-202-8886

What is meniscus repair, and can my tear be repaired?

The meniscus is the knee’s shock absorber — a pair of cartilage wedges that spread load, steady the joint and keep the surfaces off each other. Lose it and the knee wears out faster, which is why the first question is always whether it can be saved rather than trimmed.

Whether it can be repaired comes down to where the tear sits. The outer rim has a blood supply and will heal; the inner portion has almost none and generally will not. Arthroscopy shows the pattern of the tear and which zone it is in, and that decides between stitching it, trimming the damaged part, or adding biologic augmentation.

Where it can be stitched, it is. Recovery from a repair asks more of you than a trim does — protected movement first, then strength, then control, then a staged return to sport — but you keep the cartilage, and that is what protects the knee from arthritis twenty years out.

Which meniscus tears do we treat?

Acute Meniscus Tears

The tear that happens in one moment — a twist, a pivot, a bad landing.

Degenerative Meniscus Tears

Tissue that has weakened with age and frayed rather than snapped.

Bucket Handle Meniscus Tear

A large fragment folded into the joint, locking the knee or stopping it straightening.

Radial Meniscus Tears

Tears running across the fibres, which undo the meniscus’s ability to spread load.

Recurrent Meniscus Pain After Previous Surgery

Symptoms that came back after earlier surgery.

Meniscus Extrusion

A meniscus that has slipped outward from the joint line and stopped doing its job.

Meniscus Tear with ACL Injury

A meniscus tear alongside an ACL injury — the two often arrive together.

Mechanical Knee Locking / Catching

A knee that catches or locks because loose tissue is caught in the joint.

Why preserving it matters

  • The knee keeps its own shock absorber
  • Load spread across the joint the way it was meant to be
  • The pain, swelling, catching and locking settle
  • A lower chance of arthritis arriving early
  • Normal movement patterns restored
  • Done arthroscopically, through small incisions
  • Rehabilitation written to protect the repair while it heals
  • Weight-bearing and strength work reintroduced in stages
  • A supported route back to training and sport
  • The long game: keeping the joint you were born with
