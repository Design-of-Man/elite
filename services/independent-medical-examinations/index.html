Independent Medical Examinations (IME)

Objective orthopedic IMEs for insurers, employers and attorneys, from a board-certified surgeon. Serving Port St. Lucie and Palm Beach Gardens, FL.

Schedule a Consultation 561-202-8886

What is an IME, and what is it for?

An IME exists to answer a question a treating relationship cannot answer neutrally: is the injury what it is claimed to be, how severe is it, and has the treatment so far been reasonable. The examination covers the medical history, the imaging, the treatment record, and a focused physical examination where the question calls for one. What comes out is a written report — diagnosis, functional limitations, treatment recommendations, prognosis — set down in a form that will survive scrutiny. The work is not advocacy. It is not performed on behalf of the claimant, the employer, the insurer or counsel, and the findings land wherever the evidence puts them.

When is an IME requested?

Accident-Related Injury Claims

An impartial read on injuries from a road, workplace or public-liability accident.

Acute Orthopedic & Soft Tissue Trauma

Assessment of fractures, ligament and tendon damage, and soft-tissue injury.

Chronic Pain & Functional Limitation Cases

Measured findings on mobility and what the claimant can actually do day to day.

Spine, Shoulder, Knee, and Joint Pain Claims

Separating pre-existing wear from damage the incident caused.

Post-Surgical Outcome & Recovery Reviews

Whether recovery has tracked the way it should after the procedure.

Causation & Injury-Event Relationship Determination

Whether the injury and the reported event are in fact linked.

Permanent Impairment Ratings

Long-term impairment, rated against published clinical standards.

Disputed or Conflicting Medical Case Opinions

A deciding opinion where earlier assessments contradict each other.

What the report gives you

  • A neutral medical analysis, owed to no party
  • Findings explained, and supported by what was examined
  • Documentation detailed enough to hold up under challenge
  • Disputes resolved on evidence rather than attrition
  • Causation set out plainly instead of left implied
  • Functional limitation assessed accurately, not estimated
  • A defensible basis for return-to-work and care decisions
  • Inconsistencies between what is reported and what is found, noted
  • A clearer picture for counsel and insurers to work from
  • Carried out by a board-certified orthopedic surgeon

Reports are returned promptly, and written to be used.
