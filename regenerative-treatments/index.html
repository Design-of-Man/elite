What Is Regenerative Treatment?

Healing that starts at the cellular level

At Elite Sports Medicine, our regenerative treatments harness the body's natural healing power to repair and restore damaged tissue. Using advanced biologic therapies — Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP), stem cell treatments, and growth factor injections — we stimulate regeneration at the cellular level, helping your body heal itself more effectively.

Whether you're recovering from a sports injury, chronic joint pain, or tendon degeneration, regenerative medicine offers a non-surgical, evidence-based path that promotes long-term healing rather than temporary relief. Every treatment plan is personalized to your injury, age, activity level, and healing goals.

FAQ

Regenerative treatment questions, answered

Regenerative treatments are non-surgical procedures that use the body's own healing mechanisms to repair damaged tissue, including PRP, stem cell therapy, and growth factor injections. They concentrate and reintroduce natural healing components into the injured area to accelerate recovery, reduce inflammation, and improve function.

When injected into a damaged tendon, ligament, or joint, concentrated platelets or cells release growth factors that encourage tissue regeneration, collagen formation, and improved blood flow — strengthening weak or torn areas and restoring joint mechanics over time.

Regenerative treatments are considered safe when performed by trained medical professionals using sterile, evidence-based technique. Most procedures use your own biologic material, which reduces the risk of allergic reaction or rejection. Every procedure follows medical protocol and is customized to your diagnosis.

Timelines vary with the severity of the condition and your body's healing rate. Some patients notice improvement within a few weeks, while deeper tissue healing can continue over several months. Your physician will monitor progress and may recommend follow-up sessions.

Good candidates include patients with chronic joint pain, tendon or ligament injuries, mild-to-moderate arthritis, or those recovering from sports-related trauma — and anyone seeking an alternative to surgery. Your specialist will confirm fit during a consultation.
